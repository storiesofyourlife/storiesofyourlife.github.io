Tower of Babylon

Were the tower to be built in this day, it would be a matter of years only. The foundations alone would not be a task to vex a master stonecutter to distraction; for such a foundation, I could vault a small country.

But in the days of which I speak, such expertise was not to be had. Rather than employing the easy methods we know today, they raised the Tower by trial and error, by dangerous experiment. Many were the lives lost in that building.

The Tower of Babylon was begun when Hillalum was a young man. His father, Khishtu, joined the work force not long after the first foundation stones were laid, when the project was still unknown to the world. Hillalum himself was born within a year of the Tower's beginning, and he learned to climb before he learned to walk, learned to carry stones before he learned to carry food.

This opening story of Ted Chiang's collection introduces us to a world where ancient Babylonians attempt to build a tower to reach heaven. It's a masterful blend of mythological storytelling with scientific precision, exploring themes of human ambition, divine architecture, and the nature of the cosmos itself.
