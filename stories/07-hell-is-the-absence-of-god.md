# Hell Is the Absence of God

This is the story of a man named Neil Fisk, and how he came to love God. Neil's wife Sarah was one of the casualties when the angel Raguel visited the Cerritos library. She was crushed by falling debris when the library ceiling collapsed during the angel's appearance.

In this world, the existence of God is not a matter of faith—it is an observable, scientific fact. Angels appear regularly, performing miracles and sometimes causing collateral damage. Heaven and Hell are real destinations, visible during certain angelic visitations. People can sometimes see into Hell and witness the torments of the damned.

The disturbing truth is that salvation is not based on moral behavior or religious devotion. Some of the most virtuous people are damned to Hell, while some of the wicked are taken up to Heaven. Love of God is the only criterion for salvation, and this love cannot be forced or feigned—it must be genuine and spontaneous.

Neil did not love God. Even after Sarah's death, even after witnessing undeniable proof of divine existence, he could not bring himself to feel love for a being who allowed such arbitrary suffering. But Neil discovered that he might be able to change his own nature, to literally transform himself into someone capable of loving God.

Set in a world where religious faith is replaced by religious knowledge, this story explores the tension between love and justice, between divine omnipotence and human suffering. Chiang creates a universe where God's existence is certain but God's goodness is deeply questionable.

The story is both a theological thought experiment and a moving portrait of grief, examining what it means to love something that has caused you profound pain, and whether genuine love can exist without free choice.
