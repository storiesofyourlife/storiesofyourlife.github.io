# Division by Zero

Renee was looking for her husband when she found him in the denominators. Division by zero: in mathematics, the result is not a number, but rather undefined. For Renee, a mathematician, this concept was about to take on a very personal meaning.

Carl had discovered something in mathematics that should not have been possible—a proof that 1 = 0. The implications were staggering: if this proof were correct, it would mean that all of mathematics was inconsistent, that every theorem was both true and false, that the logical foundation of human knowledge was fundamentally flawed.

Renee watched her husband descend into a kind of madness born not of mental illness, but of mathematical revelation. The proof was elegant, undeniable, and absolutely devastating to anyone who understood its implications. Carl had found the fatal flaw in the logical structure of reality itself.

"Division by Zero" is a deeply personal story that parallels the collapse of a marriage with the potential collapse of mathematical certainty. Chiang weaves together the emotional breakdown of Renee and Carl's relationship with the existential crisis that would follow the discovery that mathematics—the supposed bedrock of logical thought—contains an inherent contradiction.

The story explores how we construct meaning in our lives, and what happens when the frameworks we depend on for understanding reality itself prove to be fundamentally unreliable. It's both an intimate portrait of a relationship in crisis and a philosophical examination of certainty, truth, and the foundations of knowledge.
