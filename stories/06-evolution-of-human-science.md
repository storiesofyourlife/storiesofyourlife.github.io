# The Evolution of Human Science

The history of science can be divided into two periods: Before the Metahumans and After the Metahumans. We are now in the After period, and human science as we knew it has become obsolete.

The metahumans appeared gradually, through a combination of genetic engineering and cybernetic enhancement. They are still recognizably human in origin, but their cognitive capabilities so far exceed baseline human intelligence that they might as well be a different species entirely.

For the first time in history, there are scientific papers that no unenhanced human can understand. The metahumans have moved beyond our ability to follow their reasoning, their mathematical proofs, or even their basic conceptual frameworks. We have become to them what dogs are to us—beloved, perhaps, but intellectually irrelevant.

This transformation has happened remarkably quickly, within a single generation. Human scientists who were once at the forefront of their fields now find themselves unable to contribute meaningfully to the advancement of knowledge. The questions we can ask and answer are no longer the interesting ones.

This brief but powerful story examines what happens when a subset of humanity transcends the cognitive limitations of the rest of our species. Written as a historical account, "The Evolution of Human Science" explores the implications of superhuman intelligence and the potential obsolescence of normal human intellectual capabilities.

Chiang presents this scenario without judgment, treating it as a natural evolutionary development while highlighting the profound psychological and social consequences for those left behind. It's a meditation on progress, intelligence, and what it means to be human when humanity itself is no longer the pinnacle of consciousness.
