# Liking What You See: A Documentary

[Interview transcript: Tamera Lyons, sophomore, Pembleton University] The calli procedure? Yeah, I've had it since I was twelve. Basically, it makes you blind to beauty and ugliness in faces. Everyone just looks normal to you.

[Interview transcript: Dr. Joseph Weiss, developer of calliagnosia therapy] People think beauty is this wonderful thing, but it's actually a source of tremendous inequality. Beautiful people have advantages in every area of life—employment, relationships, social status. The calli procedure levels the playing field.

[Interview transcript: Maria Santos, parent] I had the procedure done on my daughter when she was young because I wanted her to be judged for who she is, not what she looks like. I wanted her to judge others the same way. It seemed like the right thing to do.

[Interview transcript: Alex Hartley, student] The university wants to make calli mandatory for all students. They say it will eliminate lookism and create a more equitable environment. Some of us think that's going too far. Beauty isn't just about advantage—it's about art, about human experience.

Presented as a documentary with interview transcripts, this story explores a near-future technology that can selectively disable a person's ability to perceive facial beauty and ugliness. The narrative follows a campus debate about whether this "calliagnosia" treatment should be mandatory.

Chiang examines complex questions about equality, discrimination, and the value of aesthetic experience. Is beauty a social good that enriches human life, or is it primarily a source of unfair privilege that should be eliminated? The story presents compelling arguments on multiple sides while exploring the unintended consequences of technological solutions to social problems.
