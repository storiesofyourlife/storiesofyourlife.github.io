# Understand

I foresee my death in just a few minutes: Bullet fragments, from a gun held by a man named Reynolds, will sever the carotid artery in my neck, and I will die almost immediately.

This has become a rather strange situation. I have become cognitively enhanced far beyond my original capacity, and Reynolds has undergone the same treatment. We are perhaps the only two people on Earth who can truly understand each other—and we are attempting to kill one another.

My name is Leon Greco, and I am a human being. At least, I was until several months ago, when I was selected for an experimental intelligence enhancement program. The enhancement has had effects that its creators never anticipated. I can now perceive patterns in data that were previously invisible to human cognition.

In "Understand," Chiang explores the concept of super-intelligence and its consequences. The story follows Leon Greco, who receives an experimental treatment that dramatically enhances his cognitive abilities, eventually developing god-like intelligence. As his intelligence grows, he discovers another enhanced individual, leading to a conflict between two beings who have transcended normal human understanding.

The narrative examines themes of consciousness, the nature of intelligence, and what happens when humans acquire capabilities that fundamentally change their relationship with reality and with each other. It's a profound meditation on the isolation that might come with transcendent intelligence.
